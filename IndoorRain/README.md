# Indoor Weather (IndoorRain): muffled rain, snow and sandstorm under a roof

When it rains and you step under a roof, the client cuts its own rain sound completely: in a real storm in Goldshire the rain went silent in the inn and under the blacksmith's awning, as if the storm had stopped. This mod fills that silence. Inside, you hear a muffled version of whatever the weather is doing, rain, snow or sandstorm, light, medium or heavy; it fades in at the door, and it fades out the moment you step back out or the weather stops. Zone music is untouched. Since 0.13 the rain outside is a storm too: louder rain, thunder at random distances with a flash of lightning before it, wind gusts, and, with comfyatmosphere installed, thicker fog and dimmer sun shafts while it rains (see Storms below). Since 0.14 the addon ships no sound files: the DLL builds every sound it plays from the player's own client files, the rain loops as the game starts and the rest when they are first wanted (see The sounds below).

It is two pieces, the same shape as Nampower and its settings addon: a small DLL that reads the client's own weather state and plays the loop through the client's own sound engine, and an addon that supplies the indoor check and holds the settings. The nine indoor loops are the client's own weather sounds, filtered so they sound like they come through walls, and the storm's rain, thunder and wind are the client's own too; the DLL makes all of them in memory from the player's own archives, so nothing of Blizzard's audio is in this mod.

0.14 (the sounds built from the client's own archives) passed the Wine harness on 30 Sep 2026, 113 checks with none failed, against the RavenCraft client's own fmod.dll and archives, and was heard in game the same evening on the RavenCraft client under Proton, in a real thunderstorm in Goldshire: the six rain loops built in 2.1 s at start (32.1 MB held), the ten thunders and three gusts in 0.2 s when the storm came on, the game's rain loops swapped for the built ones, and the muffled rain in the inn, all from the player's own files. Natively, the MPQ reader matches a Python reference byte for byte on every source file in that client and ends in its own error code, with nothing leaked, on malformed and randomly damaged archives and on a failed read or allocation at every step (`tools/mpq_selftest.c`); every built sound matches 0.13's as the client's mixer plays it (`tools/ownsounds_selftest.c`).

Tested up to 0.13: heard in game on the RavenCraft client under Proton, in a real heavy rainstorm in Goldshire (in the inn and under the blacksmith's awning), and in rain, snow and sand forced through a test switch, entering and leaving inns. A Wine harness that boots the client's own fmod.dll with the client's globals faked in one page it maps, read there by a test build of the DLL, passed 71 checks on 0.13, including the weather state that real storm produced, the indoor level following the storm's strength, the ambience slider and the Ambient Sounds box, the game closing its sound engine under a playing loop, a full mixer trying to steal the loop's channel, and (0.13) the game's rain loop swapped on open, strikes, thunder outdoors and muffled indoors, gusts, nothing of the storm and no swap while the mod is off, the digits written into `IndoorRain_Storm` (the addon's session echoed, a strike digit that counts every strike, a value of the wrong shape and a read-only page both left byte-identical), a thunder that finds no channel on a full mixer rather than take a game sound's, and no building probe in a default ini. A simulation of the addon under a stub of the 1.12 API (Lua 5.1) drives the fog ramp, the flash and the restore paths, including `/indoorrain off` in the middle of a storm putting the player's fog values back exactly. Four hostile code reviews on the way to 0.12, the last on 0.11, and one on the 0.13 changes (29 Sep), whose findings are fixed: off did not stop the storm, the building probe ran by default, a thunder could push a game sound off a full mixer, and a game folder whose name falls outside the ANSI code page stopped the sounds loading.

In game, 0.13 (the build before those fixes) ran on the RavenCraft client on 29 Sep from 00:28 to 00:38 without trouble. Its log shows a `storm: strike` line and the `storm: thunder` that followed it, a `storm: gust`, an `outdoor rain:` line for the game's rain loop swapped for IndoorRain's, and `building:` lines naming the Goldshire inn, a dwarven inn and Stormwind by group (the probe was on by default in that build). That proves the chain runs on a real client; how it sounds is still the player's to confirm by ear. Not tested: native Windows, macOS through WoWSilicon, and real (not forced) snow or sandstorms.

For a launcher that installs mods from a catalog: one row in the shape of Nampower's (Proposed catalog row, below), pointing at the DLL and the addon zip of this repository's release. Nothing in the launcher's code changes.

## What is in this folder

- `dll/` the DLL source and `build.sh`; built with `i686-w64-mingw32-gcc`, no C runtime, imports only KERNEL32 and USER32. `indoorrain.c` and `addresses.h` are the DLL itself; since 0.14 it also builds its sounds with `mpq.c` and `inflate.c` (one file at a time out of the client's MPQ archives, read-only) and `sounds.c` and `dsp.c` (the recipes of the two scripts in `tools/`, ported to C with no C runtime), each with its header.
- `addon/IndoorRain/` the addon: `IndoorRain.toc` and `IndoorRain.lua`. Up to 0.13 it also held the 25 sounds as `.ogg` files; since 0.14 it holds none.
- `harness/` the Wine test (`harness.c`, `fmod.def`, `run.sh`, `last-run.txt`) and `display_probe.c`, the native check `run.sh` makes before Wine starts.
- `tools/` `pull_weather.py` (reads the nine loop names out of the client's `SoundEntries.dbc` and pulls the files from the archives with StormLib) `make_loops.sh` (the ffmpeg chains that made the loops up to 0.13) and `make_storm_assets.sh` (the storm sounds, and the medium and heavy indoor loops remade without thunder): since 0.14 these three only record where the DLL's recipes came from, and each refuses an output folder inside a git work tree, because what it writes is a copy of Blizzard's sounds: the folder is judged where it lands (every symlink and `..` resolved, a folder not made yet taken as the one that will be), a `.git` is looked for on disk rather than asked of git, whose failure would read as outside, and no file is written through a link at its name, a symlink or a hard link, whose other name could be in a work tree: the scripts have ffmpeg write into a folder of their own and rename each file into place, and the Python and C tools write only to a plain file with no other name. `mpq_selftest.c`, `sounds_render.c` and `ownsounds_selftest.c` are the native Linux tests of the MPQ reader, of the recipes (and of the resampler, `resampletest`), and of the DLL's whole build from a client's `Data` folder, with its faults and settings (their headers give the build lines; each reads client files from folders you name, only reads them, and writes only where you tell it, and like the scripts each refuses a folder inside a git work tree). `building_roofs.tsv` is a roof type for each building model, for the coming roof sounds by building.
- `LICENSE` GPL-3.0; License, below, says what it covers.
- `.gitignore` keeps `.ogg` and `.wav` files, in any case of the extension, out of this folder's commits.

## How it works

The DLL is loaded by VanillaFixes from `dlls.txt`. It starts one thread and does nothing at all until the client's sound engine is up (`FSOUND_GetMaxChannels() > 0`, plus two seconds); early activity coincided with start-up crashes inside another injected mod, so the DLL stays out of the client's start-up entirely. Then, every 250 ms, it reads through VirtualQuery-guarded reads:

- in world: the object manager pointer at `0x00B41414` and the active player GUID at `+0xC0`;
- weather: the `CMapWeather` pointer at `0x00C6326C` (type at `+0x20`: -1 none, 1 rain, 2 snow, 3 sand; target intensity at `+0x00`; effect object pointers at `+0x28/+0x2C/+0x30`), and the server's weather sound id at `0x00B05FBC` (rain 8533..8535, snow 8536..8538, sand 8556..8558, light/medium/heavy);
- indoors: the CVar `IndoorRain_Indoors`, written by the addon, which polls `IsIndoors()` (SuperWoW or ClassicAPI) four times a second on the game's own thread. The DLL does not work this out from the object manager.

One optional read does walk the object manager: the building probe, off by default (`building_probe=1` in the ini turns it on). It finds the player in the object manager's list and follows the chain the client's own zone text uses to the building model and group the player stands in (`dll/addresses.h`). It only reads, and all it does with the answer is write `building:` lines to the log. It is there to prove that chain on real clients before the coming roof sounds by building use it.

The weather sound id decides which weather it is and how heavy. It counts when the type field names the same weather, or when the type names none (anything outside 1..3) and the storm has intensity above zero. The type field cannot be trusted on its own: in the real Goldshire storm it read 1 for one poll and then -1 for the rest of the storm while the rain sound kept playing outdoors (8535, heavy, and 8534, medium, when the storm eased; indoors the loop switched to the medium track within a second). When the id does not count (it is not one of the nine, or the type names a different weather), the type field decides instead, but only with that weather's effect object present and an intensity of 27% or more, which picks the level (below 40% light, below 70% medium, else heavy); otherwise nothing plays. The loop follows that signal as it is: at the start of one real storm the id and intensity dropped to 0 and came back three times in about eleven seconds before holding, so a player indoors at that moment would hear the loop start and stop with it.

Settings come from the CVars `IndoorRain_Enabled` and `IndoorRain_Volume` (0 to 100, default 70), registered by the addon and read by the DLL through the client's own CVar lookup at `0x0063DEC0` (its first twelve bytes are compared before the call; the lookup runs once per CVar, three seconds after the world is up; the value string at `CVar+0x20` is read byte-guarded each poll), with `IndoorRain.ini` next to `WoW.exe` as the fallback and the place for the test switches (`force_weather=1|2|3`, `force_indoors=1`; the harness's test build also reads `data_dir`, see Building).

How loud: indoors the loop plays at `IndoorRain_Volume` percent of the outdoor level of the same weather in the game's own files, so 70 is about 3 dB down from the game's own rain loop, and muffled. Since 0.13 the rain outside is louder (see Storms) and the indoor level does not follow it up: at 70, indoors is about 12 dB under light rain outside, 10 dB under medium and 5 dB under heavy, and back to 3 dB with `outdoor_rain=0`. Outdoors the client plays each weather file at its volume in `SoundEntries.dbc` (0.69 for all nine) times the `AmbienceVolume` slider, and not at all with Ambient Sounds off; the storm's strength lives in the files themselves: the game's light rain is 14 dB quieter than its heavy rain. `MasterVolume` is applied by the sound engine to every channel, this loop's included (`FSOUND_SetSFXMasterVolume`), so the DLL leaves it out of the level; a master at zero only means there is no loop to play. The DLL reads `AmbienceVolume`, `EnableAmbience` and `MasterVolume` through the same CVar lookup and gives each loop the gain that brings it back to its original's loudness (EBU R128, measured with ffmpeg on the client's `Sound\Ambience\Weather` files and the 0.13 loops; the loops 0.14 builds come out within 0.5 dB of those, the indoor rain a little louder); the rain loops were loudness-normalised when they were made, which is why 0.11 played a light storm indoors nearly as loud as a heavy one. Ambience at zero, or Ambient Sounds off, means no loop at all. A setting that cannot be read is treated as full. A custom track set in the ini (`track_<kind>_<level>`, see The sounds) gets the built track's gain, so it should be made at the built track's loudness, integrated: -16 LUFS for rain at every level; -31.7, -29.5 and -22.5 LUFS for light, medium and heavy snow; -32.0, -28.0 and -23.6 LUFS for sand. Since 0.13 it must be a loose file in the game folder (not inside the game's archives), at most 32 MB (an ogg; a long uncompressed wav is too big), because the DLL plays every sound from its own memory (below).

Sound goes through the client's own `fmod.dll` (FMOD 3.75). Since 0.13 every sound the DLL plays is in memory it owns, opened with `FSOUND_LOADMEMORY` (since 0.14 the sounds it builds, and a custom track read once from the game folder): the client routes FMOD's file access through its own file layer, whose cache has no lock and expects to be used from the game's thread only, and opening files by name from the DLL's thread raced it (found in the 0.13 hostile review; 0.12 did it too). The archives and a custom track are read through the wide-character Windows calls, so a game folder whose name has characters outside the ANSI code page loads them too; the log and the ini are still opened by their ANSI paths, so in such a folder no log is written and the ini's defaults apply. Then: `FSOUND_Stream_PlayEx` started paused, volume set to zero and unpaused, a linear fade in and out over `fade_ms` (default 1000) whatever the level, `FSOUND_StopSound` and `FSOUND_Stream_Close`. The channel is taken at priority 255 and reserved with `FSOUND_SetReserved` while the loop plays, because the client mixes on twelve channels and a busy inn otherwise takes it back. A heavier or lighter weather, or another kind, fades out and restarts with the matching sound. Leaving the world stops it at once; volume 0, turning it off or the weather ending fade it out the same way stepping outside does.

The only writes into the client are three import-table slots in `WoW.exe` and one CVar string (see Storms). The entries for `FSOUND_Close` and `FSOUND_Init` are redirected (matched by resolved pointer, `VirtualProtect` around the store, restored if the DLL is ever unloaded) to functions that take the same critical section the worker holds around every FMOD call, so the game closing or restarting its sound engine can never race a live stream (WoW 1.12.1 starts it once, at launch, and closes it once, at exit; only the harness restarts it). No FMOD 3.75 query changes after `FSOUND_Close`, which is why this is done by interception rather than by polling.

Every read of client memory is a copy through `ReadProcessMemory` on the game's own process, and the few digits written into `IndoorRain_Storm` go through `WriteProcessMemory`, so a region the game releases mid-read makes a read fail rather than crash the game (0.13 re-review; earlier versions checked with `VirtualQuery` and then read directly, which leaves a gap).

Log: `Logs\IndoorRain.log` next to `WoW.exe`, capped at 1 MB, never an absolute path.

## The sounds, built from the client's own files (0.14)

Up to 0.13 the addon shipped its 25 sounds as `.ogg` files, made offline from the client's own sound files by `tools/make_loops.sh` and `tools/make_storm_assets.sh`. Those are copies of Blizzard audio and cannot go on a public site, so since 0.14 the addon ships none: the DLL reads the same client files out of the player's own archives and runs the same recipes itself, in memory.

- **Where from.** The MPQs in the `Data` folder next to `WoW.exe`, opened read-only and shared for reading and writing (the game has them open too), with plain Windows file calls on the DLL's own thread, never through the client's own archive code; each is closed again after its file. `patch.MPQ`: the original game's light and medium rain loops, which have no thunder in them, and the snow and sandstorm loops. `patch-S.mpq`: RavenCraft's 48 kHz heavy rain loop, the close thunder (`CallLightning.wav`) and the three shaman wind casts the gusts are made from. `sound.MPQ`: the four Blasted Lands lightning bolts. `patch-S.mpq`'s medium rain loop, which has thunder recorded into it, is never used. The heavy loop is taken only at exactly 14,419,190 bytes, the file its splice times were measured on; any other heavy loop gets no splice (below). A file the recipes were not made for is refused before a sample of it is decoded, for the session: over 90 seconds, over 32 MB as samples, or at a rate outside 8 to 96 kHz, and for the gusts an Ogg whose own pages do not give the length the client's decoder will find (a changed archive could otherwise hand the DLL a 64 MB WAV that takes 256 MB as samples, or an Ogg that FMOD would decode whole into the game's own heap). The gust's length is read from its pages, every one of them in order: each whole, with its checksum right and the first page's serial number, one stream begun on the first page and ended on the last, and nothing after it; a second stream chained on, or page-like bytes after the end, which the length used to be taken from, are refused. Every real source is at most 75 seconds and 27.5 MB. An archive whose name differs only in case (`PATCH-S.MPQ`) is found too. Only a file that is not there moves on to the next archive in its list: no such archive, no such file in it, a stub of size zero, or the heavy loop at another size. Anything else stops at the archive the client would read it from too (damaged data, a lack of memory, an archive another program holds or that reads short), so no sound is ever built from an older copy in another archive because the first could not be read just then; see The log for what happens instead. The reader (`dll/mpq.c`, `dll/inflate.c`) takes MPQ format 0 and 1 below 4 GB with zlib sectors, which is what every source is, checks every offset and size against the archive before using it, and refuses anything else rather than guess.
- **The recipes.** `dll/sounds.c` on the building blocks of `dll/dsp.c`: the ffmpeg chains of the two scripts, ported to C with no C runtime, 16-bit, as a WAV image in memory.
- **The rate.** Every sound is built at the rate the game's mixer runs at, which the DLL asks FMOD for (`FSOUND_GetOutputRate`; the game's `SoundMixRate` setting picks it, and both clients run 44,100 Hz), because FMOD 3.75's software mixer, the one both clients' `Sound.log` name (`MMXP6`), does not interpolate a 16-bit stereo voice at another rate: it holds each frame until the next is due. The indoor loop, the thunder and the gusts are opened as software voices, and so is the game's own rain loop wherever there are no hardware voices, as under Wine or Proton without DSOAL and on Windows since Vista. The first 0.14 build took the muffled sounds to lower rates to save memory, and the mixer's hold put a hiss of 5.5 to 14 kHz over the indoor rain, 16 to 27 dB over 0.13's there, and a fizz above 11 kHz over the outdoor rain at its source's 22,050 Hz; it dropped frames of RavenCraft's 48 kHz heavy loop with nothing to stop its top folding down, and a mono voice it draws on a straight line between frames, which dulled the thunder at 22,050 Hz. At the mixer's rate each loop is stepped one frame per frame, as 0.13's files were. The sounds are built for mixers of 8 to 48 kHz: `FSOUND_Init` takes 4,000 to 65,535 Hz, and a mixer set past 48 kHz by hand gets them at 48 kHz, whose frames it plays once or twice, unevenly, folding images of their band back down to 12.5 kHz and up, as it would 0.13's 44.1 kHz files; building them faster would take memory in step with the rate, and the log names both rates. Snow and sand keep their files' 22,050 Hz while the mixer runs at twice that: 0.13 shipped them so, and that is how they were heard and approved; their band ends near 750 Hz, so the images the hold makes of it land at 20 kHz and up, as with 0.13 (played through a model of that mixer, 28.8 to 34.5 dB under the whole loop, within 0.1 dB of 0.13's own), and at 44.1 kHz the six would take twice the memory. The thunder and the gusts are the other exception. The DLL plays them at a random pitch (88 to 104 % and 90 to 110 %), so the mixer does not step them one frame per frame but draws a straight line between two frames of the mono voice, which dulls the top of their band and folds images of it back into what is heard, the more the larger a share of the voice's rate the band is: built at a 22,050 Hz mixer's own rate, thunder_close came out 3.6 dB duller at 8 kHz than the 44.1 kHz build played on that mixer, with images and aliases 14 dB louder (-23 dB of the whole against -37). So they are built at the least multiple of the mixer's rate that is 44.1 kHz or more (the mixer's own from 44.1 kHz up, twice it at 22,050 Hz, four times at 11,025 Hz), cut to the mixer's band on the way; as the mixer plays them, at every pitch the DLL gives them and at each of seven mixer rates tried from 8 to 48 kHz, each is then no duller than the 44.1 kHz build played on the same mixer and, where they are within 60 dB of the whole, has no more images and aliases, and at 22,050 Hz the ten thunders are that build, byte for byte. Each source is resampled by a windowed sinc (`dsp_resample`), flat to 90% of the lower Nyquist frequency and 90 dB down from it up, and each recipe runs on the side of that step the scripts ran it: the outdoor rain after it, at the mixer's rate; the medium and heavy indoor rain after it too, at 44.1 kHz, where the scripts ran them, even for a slower mixer, or at the mixer's own rate where that is faster (at 48 kHz that moves them 0.5 dB from the 44.1 kHz way, at 10 kHz), and only then at the mixer's rate (run at 22,050 Hz their 2.5 kHz low-pass left them 7.7 dB duller at 8 kHz than when built for 44.1 kHz), RavenCraft's heavy loop spliced first at its own 48 kHz; the light indoor rain, the thunder and the gusts before it, at their sources' rates (a 2-pole low-pass at 2.5 kHz is a different filter at 22,050 Hz, and run at 44.1 kHz it left the light indoor rain 7 dB brighter at 8 kHz than 0.13's). Loops are filtered and resampled as loops, so the loop point carries no step of the recipes' making. The outdoor medium loop keeps the one Blizzard's own `RainMediumLoop.wav` has at its loop point, 5.1 times the loop's median sample step (7.5 times in the 0.13 file).
- **The gusts.** They are Ogg Vorbis, which only the client's own `fmod.dll` can decode here: it decodes each one into a sample of its own, whose samples the DLL copies out before freeing it, under the same lock as every FMOD call of the DLL.
- **When.** Nothing is built that cannot play. The rain loops the settings want (with the defaults, the three outdoor loops and the three indoor ones) are built at start, once the sound engine is up and the hooks are in, before the DLL first looks at the world: the game asks for its rain loop within a quarter of a second of entering a rainy world, and the swap needs its loop by then. Nothing is built while the mod is off, and no outdoor loop with `outdoor_rain=0`. The storm's ten thunders and three gusts wait for its switch, which needs the addon connected: they are built at the poll the storm comes on, before its first strike is due (the thunders also for a test strike with the storm off). A snow or sand loop is built the first time it is wanted indoors, and its loop starts once it is built. Whatever a setting turns on later is built at the poll it comes on, and the swap opens only once its loops are built. Measured natively with the DLL's own 32-bit x87 arithmetic, on a fast desktop CPU and the RavenCraft client, with the mixer at the clients' 44.1 kHz: the six rain loops at start in 2.1 s, the storm's thirteen sounds in 0.27 s (with ffmpeg in FMOD's place for the gusts' decode), each snow or sand loop in 25 to 50 ms, all 25 in 2.6 s. The times follow the mixer's rate: at 48 kHz, where snow and sand are resampled too, 1.7 s at start, 0.41 s for the storm, 0.26 to 0.39 s for each snow or sand loop, and 4.2 s for all 25; at 22,050 Hz, 2.0 s at start (the medium and heavy indoor rain are made at 44.1 kHz first), 0.34 s for the storm (its thunder and gusts go to 44.1 kHz, twice the mixer's rate) and 2.6 s for all 25. A slower CPU takes longer, and a Mac under Rosetta, which has to emulate the x87 arithmetic, will be much slower still; nothing has been measured there, and the first log from Wine or a real client will say. Until a rain loop is built, or if it cannot be, the game plays its own (see Storms); the swap takes over the next time the game opens its rain loop.
- **Memory.** Measured natively as above: the six rain loops hold 32.1 MB from the start, the rain set with the storm's sounds 36.7 MB, and all 25 61.9 MB, of which the six snow and sand loops are 25.2 MB (16.7, 19.1 and 31.7 MB in the first 0.14 build, at the lower rates the mixer played with a hiss). The most the builds hold at once is 57.0 MB at start, while RavenCraft's heavy loop is built (its decoded 75-second source alone is 27.5 MB); a later build takes at most 25 MB on top of what is held, 82.1 MB in all at the last snow or sand loop's build with every other sound held. The whole native test peaked at 84 MB resident. These are for the mixer at 44.1 kHz, and they follow its rate, which the game's `SoundMixRate` setting picks: at 48 kHz the six rain loops hold 35.0 MB (50.5 MB at the start's peak, the heavy loop not being resampled), the rain set 39.9 MB, all 25 94.8 MB, of which snow and sand, resampled to 48 kHz too, are 54.9 MB; a snow or sand loop's build then takes up to 43 MB on top, 126.9 MB in all at the last, and the native test peaked at 129 MB resident. At 22,050 Hz the six hold 16.1 MB (53.2 MB at the start's peak) and all 25 45.8 MB, the thunder and the gusts 4.5 MB of it at 44.1 kHz (2.2 MB more than at the mixer's own rate, see The rate above). MB here are the log's, 1,048,576 bytes. A built sound is never changed or freed until the game exits, because a stream opened on it, the game's own outdoor rain among them, reads it in place for as long as it plays.
- **The log.** One line naming the `Data` folder and the three archives with their sizes; one naming the rate of the game's mixer the sounds are built for (`the game's mixer runs at 44100 Hz: each sound is built at that rate, ...`; for a mixer under 44.1 kHz it names the thunder's and the gusts' rate too, `the game's mixer runs at 22050 Hz: each sound is built at that rate, the thunder and the gusts at 44100 Hz, ...`; for a mixer past 48 kHz it names both rates, `the game's mixer runs at 64000 Hz: each sound is built at 48000 Hz, the highest rate they are built for, ...`; and when fmod.dll cannot say, `the game's mixer rate is not known (...): each sound is built at 44100 Hz, the game's default ...`), and the game's `SoundMixRate` setting takes effect when the client starts: WoW 1.12.1 starts its sound engine once, at launch, and closes it only at exit, so the rate read at the first build holds for the session. Only when the engine is restarted at another rate through the game's import slots, which the harness does (check 17) and the game never does, is there one more line with the next build (`the game's mixer now runs at ...`), and what is built from then on is built for it; one line per client file read (its archive, size, frames, rate and time) and per sound built (the same, and its size); `sounds built at start: 6 of 6 in ... ms; ... MB held, ... MB at the build's peak; ...` after the start, and `sounds held now: ...` after each later build. Whatever cannot be built is one `cannot build` line naming the sounds and what each archive said, and that sound does not play; everything else does. A missing archive or file, damaged data, or a file the recipes were not made for, is final for the session. An archive that is there but could not be opened or read (another program holding it, a denied open, a short read) is tried again a minute later, quietly, for as long as it lasts. A lack of the DLL's own memory is tried again a minute later too, three times in all, and then given up for the session with one more line: each try reads and decodes its source again, so a client short of address space would otherwise be taken to its limit every minute, the game's own allocations with it. Until then nothing is built from another archive's copy instead: the heavy rain waits for `patch-S.mpq` rather than taking `patch.MPQ`'s older loop. Such an archive's Windows error is logged once in the session (`cannot open patch-S.mpq in the Data folder just now (Windows error 32)`, or in the first line when the scan already met it). Memory that FMOD allocates is another matter: the client gives FMOD its own heap (its `Logs\Sound.log` says `memory system configured: SMem wrappers`), and that heap ends the game when it cannot supply a block rather than fail the allocation, for the game's own sounds as for these. So the gusts' decode (about 0.4 MB for a moment each) and every stream the DLL or the swap opens can end the game in a client at the end of its address space, as any sound the game opens there can, and that is never logged or tried again.
- **Another client.** Without `patch-S.mpq` there are no gusts, the close thunder is `sound.MPQ`'s older crack, and heavy rain is `patch.MPQ`'s original 25-second loop, only made seamless: 23.5 s. With an empty or foreign `Data` folder nothing is built, nothing plays, and the game keeps its own rain loops.

The indoor tracks in the ini still work as before. `track_<kind>_<level>` defaults to `builtin`, the built sound for that weather. The nine paths 0.10 to 0.13 wrote as defaults (`Interface\AddOns\IndoorRain\indoor_<kind>_<level>.ogg`) still mean the built sound each names, even if an old copy of the file is still in the folder, so an existing `IndoorRain.ini` needs no edit. Anything else is a custom file in the game folder, read once into memory as in 0.13 (How loud, above).

## Storms (0.13)

RavenCraft's medium and heavy rain loops have thunder recorded into them on a fixed cycle (a roll 7 to 13 seconds into every 31-second medium loop, two rolls in the 75-second heavy one), so the sky rumbled like clockwork, and the rain itself was quiet next to the muffled loop indoors. 0.13 changes the rain outside and adds a storm:

- **Louder rain, no clockwork thunder.** A third import-table slot, `FSOUND_Stream_Open`, is redirected. When the game opens `RainLightLoop.wav`, `RainMediumLoop.wav` or `RainHeavyLoop.wav` (by file name, any folder, any case, and only as a plain file open), it gets IndoorRain's outdoor loop instead (since 0.14 built in memory, at start or at the poll the swap comes on, see The sounds, and handed over with `FSOUND_LOADMEMORY`, a fresh stream on the same image each time); every other file, a loop not built yet or not at all, and any failure to open ours, goes to the real call unchanged. The game still plays it at its own 0.69 times `AmbienceVolume`. Light and medium are the original game's thunder-free loops, heavy is RavenCraft's with both rolls cut out, brought to -21, -17 and -14 LUFS: light about 9 dB and medium about 7 dB louder than before, heavy about 2 dB, because the heavy rain has to leave room for the thunder, which cannot play above full channel volume. The medium and heavy indoor loops are remade from the same thunder-free sources at the same loudness as before, so indoors sounds as it did. `outdoor_rain=0` in the ini leaves the game's own loops alone, and so does the mod turned off or the addon missing; either applies the next time the game opens a rain loop. This slot is hooked on the game's own thread; the replacement takes no lock, never waits for a build, and reads only constants, one flag and the loop's address, which the DLL publishes once the loop is complete.
- **Thunder.** While it rains, strikes land at random: every 45 to 150 s in light rain, 25 to 90 s in medium, 12 to 50 s in heavy, with now and then a second strike close behind in a heavy storm. Each strike picks a distance (close, near, mid, far, farther; heavy rain brings them closer), and the thunder follows the strike by that distance's delay, 0.2 to 4 seconds, at a random pan and a slightly random pitch. Outdoors it plays at up to full volume times `AmbienceVolume`; under a roof it is a muffled copy at `IndoorRain_Volume` percent of that. Five recordings (the client's own lightning sounds), so no two storms repeat.
- **Wind.** Gusts outdoors every 40 to 90 s in light rain down to 15 to 45 s in heavy, one of three, panned and pitched at random.
- **Lightning.** The addon flashes the sky when a strike lands: a blue-white wash over the world and under the interface, two or three pulses in about a third of a second, brighter for a close strike, faint indoors. `/indoorrain flash off` turns it off.
- **Fog.** Since addon 0.14.1 the storm's fog is made by the Azeroth Atmosphere addon, so every fog decision in the pack is made in one place: it reads the rain level from `IndoorRain_Storm` and thickens comfyatmosphere's mist on top of the zone's own (held back for now: +5, +10, +15 for light, medium, heavy) while the sun shafts, volumetric light, shadows and sunlight weaken (to 70%, 45%, 25% and so on), then eases everything back when the rain ends. `/indoorrain fog on|off` and the Atmosphere window's "Thicker fog in storms" set its switch. Up to 0.14 this addon made the fog itself (+12, +22, +32 to `comfyFogThickness`, later +15, +30, +50 to the mist); Azeroth Atmosphere keeps that older storm fog switched off, so the two never both add a storm, and 0.14.1 only puts back a player's own values that a 0.14 storm was holding at the last logout.

How the two halves talk. The addon registers `IndoorRain_Storm` with the fixed-width value `IRS1:k0:l0:n000:d00` and never sets it again (it resets it only at load, and only if its shape is wrong, which the DLL never writes). The DLL overwrites single digits in place: `k` the distance of the last strike, `l` the rain level, the first two digits of `n` an echo of the addon's session code, the last digit of `n` the strike counter, `d` the delay to the thunder in tenths of a second. Before every write it re-reads the value pointer and checks the whole template, the terminator and that the page is writable, so the string keeps its size; the strike digit goes last, as one byte, so the addon sees either the old strike or the new one complete. At load the addon picks a session code (`IndoorRain_Session`) and trusts the rain level only once the DLL has echoed it, so a value saved mid-storm in `Config.wtf`, or a missing DLL, never thickens the fog. The other direction uses ordinary CVars the addon sets: `IndoorRain_Storms` (the storm switch) and `IndoorRain_Thunder` (a counter; each change is a test strike).

Settings: `/indoorrain storm on|off`, `flash on|off`, `thunder` (a test strike, with or without rain), `status`. In `IndoorRain.ini`: `storm` (the switch for an addon older than 0.5, which has none of its own; the addon was 0.5 alongside DLL 0.13, and from 0.14 it carries the DLL's number), `outdoor_rain`, `thunder_volume` and `gust_volume` (percent), `storm_rate` (percent; 200 doubles how often strikes and gusts come), and `building_probe` (0; 1 logs the building you stand in, see How it works). The thunder and gusts are one-shot streams at priority 0, the lowest, on unreserved channels: on a full mixer a thunder or gust finds no channel, or a game sound takes its channel, and it never takes a game sound's channel. The indoor loop keeps its reserved channel.

Off means off, as in 0.12. `/indoorrain off` stops the indoor loop, the thunder and gusts at once (a thunder still due after its flash is dropped, and `/indoorrain thunder` does nothing), the flash, and the fog, whose player values go back at once, as they do at logout; the game opens its own rain loops again. Without the addon connected there is no storm at all, since its switch, the flash and the fog live there.

## Installing by hand

From a release, follow the repository's README: its zip unzips into the game folder. From source, build
`IndoorRain.dll` with `dll/build.sh` (Building) and take the addon from `addon/IndoorRain/` as it is.

1. Copy `IndoorRain.dll` next to `WoW.exe`.
2. Put the addon in `Interface\AddOns\` so that `Interface\AddOns\IndoorRain\IndoorRain.toc` exists. Coming from 0.13, the 25 `.ogg` files in that folder are no longer used and can be deleted, and an existing `IndoorRain.ini` keeps working as it is.
3. Add a line `IndoorRain.dll` to `dlls.txt`. VanillaFixes shows a one-time reminder that the DLL list changed.
4. In game: `/indoorrain status`, `/indoorrain on|off`, `/indoorrain volume 0-100` (the percent of the weather outside that plays indoors, default 70). To hear it without waiting for weather, set `force_weather=1` (rain), `2` (snow) or `3` (sand) in `IndoorRain.ini`; it is re-read every two seconds. Set it back to `0` afterwards.

## Building

`dll/build.sh` on Linux with `mingw-w64-gcc` installed produces `IndoorRain.dll` (about 80 KB since 0.14, from the five sources in `dll/`; 33 KB up to 0.13).

The harness in one step: `harness/run.sh <client folder>`, for example `harness/run.sh ~/Games/RavenCraft`, with `--build-only` before or after the folder to stop before Wine; it reads every argument, and stops before building anything at one it does not know or at a second folder. It builds everything below into a fresh temporary folder, copies the client's `fmod.dll` there, runs the two walks under Wine in a prefix of its own, and prints where it put `last-run.txt` and the DLL's two logs; its exit code is the number of failed checks. The client folder is only read. Headless means no display in reach at all, which unsetting `DISPLAY` and `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` does not give: Wine's Wayland driver then tries `wayland-0` in `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, which on a Wayland desktop is the user's own session, where a first-run window or a crash dialog would open (and a dialog would stall the run). So Wine gets no `DISPLAY`, `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` or `WAYLAND_SOCKET`, an empty `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` of the run's own (which keeps the sound servers' sockets out of reach too), and its X11 and Wayland drivers disabled, and `harness/display_probe.c`, built and run natively in that same environment, must reach no display before Wine starts (`--build-only` runs it as well). `winemenubuilder` is disabled too, so a new prefix writes no menu entries or file associations into the user's home. A walk still running after 15 minutes is stopped and counts as one failed check (the harness writes its output unbuffered, so the file shows every check up to the one that hung); the prefix (about 380 MB, in the temporary folder) is deleted when the script ends, on Ctrl-C and on a TERM or HUP too, at once, and another of those while it is being deleted does not stop the deletion (a KILL leaves it, since nothing can catch one); and `last-run.txt` names the run, client and home folders as `<run>`, `<client>` and `~`, so it can be copied over `harness/last-run.txt` as it is. `harness/last-run.txt` is still the 0.13 run: the 0.14 harness has not been run yet. Its checks follow what 0.14 builds when: six rain loops at start and the storm's thirteen sounds when its switch comes on (checks 0f3, 0g, 0h, 5b2, 20h and 20i, and N1, N1c and N5f on the empty folder), each at the mixer's rate, read from FMOD (0f4, 0f5: the engine starts at 44,100 Hz, the DLL's own default too, so 0f4 takes only the line of a rate FMOD gave; check 17 restarts it at 48,000 Hz, a restart the game itself never makes, to show that the rate is read from FMOD, and 20j and 20k expect the storm's sounds, built after that, at 48,000 Hz); N5g and N5h hold an archive open against the DLL, as another program would, and expect only a wait. The full walk knows two clients and tells them apart by the DLL's scan line: RavenCraft's, whose `patch-S.mpq` gives the 45.5 s heavy splice and three gusts, and a plain 1.12.1 client without one, where checks 19, 20c and 20i expect patch.MPQ's 23.5 s heavy loop and no gust.

By hand: `harness/harness.c` builds with the same compiler against an import library made from `fmod.def` (`i686-w64-mingw32-dlltool --no-leading-underscore -d fmod.def -l libfmod.a`), and runs under Wine from one folder that holds `harness.exe`, a test build of `IndoorRain.dll` and a copy of the client's `fmod.dll`, started with that folder as the working directory; since 0.14 it needs no addon folder and no sound file. Its argument is RavenCraft's or a plain 1.12.1 client's `Data` folder as Wine names it (`harness.exe Z:\home\...\RavenCraft-fogtest\Data`; without one it takes `data_dir` from an `IndoorRain.ini` already in its folder), which it writes to `IndoorRain.ini` as `data_dir`: a test switch only the test build reads, for a folder that holds no client files. The release DLL has no such switch and always reads the `Data` folder next to `WoW.exe`. The DLL opens that folder's archives read-only, and the harness never opens them at all. `harness.exe --no-data` is the second walk, on an empty folder the harness makes in its own folder, `no-data\` (the DLL takes it as its `Data` folder; the harness itself refuses a folder holding one named `Data`, below): nothing built, each failure logged once, nothing played or swapped, no crash; and from the storm on, a `patch-S.mpq` in it that the harness holds open with no sharing, as another program would, which must cost the close thunder and the gusts only a wait, with the Windows error logged once (N5g, N5h). Since 0.14 the harness also waits for the DLL's build line instead of a fixed time (and, in the full walk, asks for a rain loop while the build runs, which must come straight back: check 0e; the empty folder's build is over a few ms after the hook goes in, too soon to ask during it), makes its own flood sample in memory, checks that each swapped rain loop is the built one by its length, and hands FMOD file callbacks of its own, then checks that none was ever called off its main thread, which stands for the game's: the client's own callbacks keep a cache with no lock. The test build is not the release DLL: three of the client's globals move to stand-in addresses in a page the harness maps (the weather gate already sits in that page at its real address), and the CVars come from a table the harness fills: `dll/build.sh <dir>/IndoorRain.dll -DADDR_CMAPWEATHER_PTR=0x00C7B100u -DADDR_WEATHER_SOUND_ID=0x00C7B200u -DADDR_OBJMGR_PTR=0x00C7B300u -DHARNESS_CVARS=0x00C7B700u`, where `<dir>` is absolute or relative to `dll/` (the script changes into `dll/` first). The harness deletes `IndoorRain.ini` and `Logs\IndoorRain.log` in its folder when it starts, because some checks count log lines and check 0d needs the ini's defaults, so never run it in a game folder; since 0.14 it refuses to start in a folder that holds `WoW.exe` or a `Data` folder. Build `harness.exe` with `-O0` (`i686-w64-mingw32-gcc -O0 -Wall -o harness.exe harness.c -L. -lfmod`): at `-O2` gcc loads the `FSOUND_Init` import address once, at the first call, and calls through that saved copy after the DLL has redirected the slot, so checks 17, 17b and 18 fail against a correct DLL. The shipped DLLs are `./build.sh ../release/0.12/IndoorRain.dll` and `./build.sh ../release/0.13/IndoorRain.dll`, run in `dll/`; ld picks the image base from the output path as written, so a build through any other output path, even an absolute path to the same file, differs in the image base and every relocated address, not only in the timestamp and checksum.

## Where the loops come from

`tools/pull_weather.py` reads `DBFilesClient\SoundEntries.dbc` from the client's archives and pulls the nine files it names (`Sound\Ambience\Weather\Rain{Light,Medium,Heavy}Loop.wav`, `Snow{Light,Medium,Heavy}.wav`, `SandStorm{Light,Medium,Heavy}.wav`) into a folder outside any git work tree. `tools/make_loops.sh` filters them: rain loses the rumble below 160 Hz (the thunder) and everything above 2.5 kHz, has its claps compressed and its level normalised; snow and sand get a 750 Hz low-pass. `tools/make_storm_assets.sh` made the storm's sounds the same way. Up to 0.13 those scripts made the `.ogg` files the addon shipped: filtered copies of the client's own sounds, which belong with the client's own distribution, not on a public site, and that is why 0.14 ships none. The scripts stay here as the record of where the recipes came from: `dll/sounds.c` runs the same ones in the DLL, on the player's own files, and its header lists where its results differ from the old files, and why.
